# Comic Relief Post-Event Survey

## Goal

Validate Comic Relief's hypothesis that comedy can drive both action and narrative change around child poverty.

## Distribution

Survey will be distributed via SMS to participants who opt in during registration. Rather than sending a link, survey questions will be asked directly via SMS to improve response rates.

## Survey Questions

### Preamble

> Thank you for attending our event! We're trying to learn whether events like these are a helpful tool to address child poverty, and would like to ask you 3-5 quick questions. We'd appreciate it if you could take the time to answer. We won't text you again once the survey is complete. You can also text STOP at any time to opt out.

### Question 1

> How likely are you to discuss the work being done by an organization present at this event with people in your life? Reply 1 for Very Likely .... 5 for Very Unlikely

**Why this question?**

Several studies, most notably a prominent study on smoking cessation, argue that communicating a narrative to others is both impactful in terms of narrative change and increases the likelihood that the communicator will identify with the narrative and take action. This makes it a stronger indicator than measures which ask how much a participant found a narrative compelling and impactful.

The format of "How likely are you to discuss X with people in your life?" is fairly standard. Making X "this event" misses whether the narrative about child poverty is what's being discussed. The goal is to ground in the context of the event while keeping a clear focus on the narrative.

**Possible Alternative/Addition**

This item measures how likely someone is to share a narrative, but it doesn't measure a shift in attitudes or belief systems. If there is a particular shift we want to measure we can add it (e.g. "How much do you agree with the statement 'I feel I could take action around child poverty and make a difference'?"). Measuring this kind of shift cleanly would require both a pre- and post-survey, which is doable but adds complexity.

### Question 2

> How likely are you to volunteer with or donate to an organization that was present at this event in the next three months? Reply 1 for Very Likely .... 5 for Very Unlikely

**Why this question?**

This is a fairly standard collective action intention question, in this case focused on willingness to engage with partner organizations. "That you learned about at this event" is intentionally included to filter out people who were active with partner organizations before the event and will continue to do so, though it potentially misses people who knew about organizations before, were inactive, and will become active because of the event.

**Possible Alternative**

> How likely are you to volunteer or donate to an organization working to address child poverty in the next three months? Reply 1 for Very Likely .... 5 for Very Unlikely

This version is more expansive. It captures people who were involved before and will continue to be involved, as well as people who were aware of partner organizations and are newly activated.

### Question 3

> How likely are you to have a followup conversation with someone that you met at this event? Reply 1 for Very Likely .... 5 for Very Unlikely

**Why this question?**

This is a measure of relationality: did the event spur conversations that were compelling enough that people want to follow up on them? This establishes the value of the event, and constructs a case that events are building community in ways that could have longer-term impact.

### Question 4 — asked only if Question 3 is 1 or 2

> How likely would you have been to meet or get to know this person if not for tonight's event? Reply 1 for Very Likely .... 5 for Very Unlikely

**Why this question?**

This is a bridging measure layered on top of the relationality measure.

### Question 5 — asked only if Question 1 is 1 or 2

> When you tell others about this event, what moment are you most likely to share?

**Why this question?**

This will highlight which parts of the event were especially emotionally impactful to inform future event planning and narrative work. This is the only open-ended question, so it comes last.

### Thanks

> That's it! Thanks so much for taking the time to complete this survey and for attending.
